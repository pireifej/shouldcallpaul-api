---
name: Delivered workshop catalog policy
description: Why the public offerings catalog is evidence-based and separate from both CRM history and résumé events.
---

The user wants unique, reusable topic offerings supported by actual public
completed workshop history, not a catalog of venues, clients, or proposed talks.
Never use private CRM records as catalog evidence or copy event descriptions
into offering copy. Preserve explicit manual hide/archive decisions.

**Why:** Maintaining offerings separately from delivered workshops created
duplicate maintenance and allowed unprepared proposed topics to appear publicly.
The user explicitly requested automatic upkeep without visitor-triggered writes
or recurring manual refreshes.

**How to apply:** Match explicit IDs, remembered decisions, known aliases and
safe exact-title normalization only. Leave uncertain equivalences for review,
including whether individual series parts establish a full-series offering.
New topic copy must be supplied as reusable public copy in the workshop save
flow. Public completion is affirmative metadata, never inferred from an old
date or a private CRM status. Keep the separate website's cache limitation
distinct from backend freshness.

## User-confirmed offering boundaries

General public speaking and Find Your Voice / Improv are separate reusable
offerings. Group the general public-speaking deliveries across venues together.
Keep the separate improv material within Find Your Voice / Improv, rather than
advertising it as a third offering in this group. This does not combine unrelated
specialties such as storytelling, elevator speeches, or de-escalation.

The full Young Speakers Academy and Youth Leadership Program share one offering.
A single youth guest presentation is not evidence of delivering the full program.
The five AI Decoded sessions represent one full-series offering, not five
additional catalog products.

**Why:** The user explicitly chose these boundaries when resolving historical
catalog links; event names and delivery formats alone do not establish how the
user wants reusable offerings grouped.

**How to apply:** Preserve these groupings in later catalog edits. If a historical
event's title conflicts with the user's account of what material was delivered,
ask which offering it belongs to instead of inferring equivalence from its name.
